It includes returning stored elastic energy, varying hardware characteristics and separate stability/structural analysis. This supports retaining force/work/stroke and footprint motion. Undefined polar source geometry must be refused instead of selecting an arbitrary terrain meridian.

Per-foot response uses explicit stiffness, damping, stroke and load limits. Unilateral normal force cannot pull a foot into the ground. Effective damping work must account for force clipping during release; a raw damping coefficient times squared speed is insufficient when that force was not applied. Tangential sliding friction is bounded by the normal load, with an explicit smooth low-speed scale and retained dissipated work. Static friction, inertia/attitude, multi-foot stability and actual coupled integration are still required before attachment or stock transfer.

Integrate body translation/orientation, finite thrust/fuel and contact forces with a supported stability/work bound. ## Coupling and compatibility requirements

The core already has an f64 Hamilton quaternion, but its convenience axis-angle/slerp methods use ordinary trigonometry. New authoritative rotation updates must construct their increments with `DMath` and use only deterministic quaternion arithmetic; do not reuse presentation interpolation in simulation. Retain explicit orientation and angular momentum/inertia assumptions, per-foot offsets, moving-surface relative velocity and off-centre torque. Finite fuel removal must disclose its inertia/exhaust-angular-momentum model rather than accidentally spinning up a uniformly scaling body.

A gear force is applied at the actual foot point. Centre force work plus torque work must equal foot-point force work; the ground-frame difference and elastic/dissipative terms must remain visible. Multi-foot load distribution, attitude/slope/footprint stability and structural/resultant load limits are required before supporting attachment. New world/scheduler/save/replay paths must deliberately route the new contact model while leaving every original version-1 manifest and absent optional field exact. Original source-edit stopping and bounded high-warp work cannot be bypassed by contact mode.

The current foot sample combines the actual moving surface, posed foot velocity and off-centre force/torque. Its reported work residual is an instantaneous identity for the fitted local tangent law. Changing normals/curvature and re-evaluated compression can change stored energy across a finite step; the coupled integrator must retain that actual storage difference and integration residual, not declare a full energy ledger from the instantaneous identity alone. A supported small-stroke/curvature regime and convergence cases are still required.

## Coupled operator being implemented

MIT's [16.07 lecture 28](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-07-dynamics-fall-2009/5e1d8699338146e5127080b880b906d6_MIT16_07F09_Lec28.pdf), pp. 1–3, establishes the centre-of-mass angular momentum and body-frame Euler equations, including the gyroscopic cross term. The implementation uses these equations in authored principal axes, with deterministic quaternion increments to track orientation. This source establishes mechanics, not numerical step accuracy or spacecraft calibration.

**Explicit engineering assumptions:** diagonal inertia is current retained mass times fixed squared gyration radii, satisfying the inertia triangle inequalities. Symmetric co-rotating propellant removal carries the corresponding angular momentum; central thrust retains the existing inertial command direction and applies no lever torque. This variable-mass extension is an authored inference, not a measured tank/engine model. The operator retains exhaust angular momentum and finite integration residuals.

The new capability uses at most 10,000 previewed substeps per one-second world task. Explicit midpoint force/torque sampling couples translation and Euler rotation; finite-mass thrust displacement and velocity remain exact for each represented command. Step bounds include authored maximum step, conservative coupled stiffness/damping leverage, rotation, local foot travel, stroke, command boundaries and fuel exhaustion. A spherical hull and other-source swept bound fail explicitly where no collision response exists. Work contains actual elastic-energy differences and numerical balance residuals; convergence must be checked against smaller steps before claiming useful landing results. Smooth sliding friction still does not support persistent attachment or cargo transfer.

Geographic terrain contact uses an explicit `cosmogon-geographic-contact-start/1` template. Signed spherical-base altitude is permitted only by that format, with a positive total radius and actual terrain/hull/foot clearance checked at creation. Original geographic arrival starts retain their positive-altitude/spherical-boundary rule. CLI hardware alignment and native contact staging use the actual geographic pose; no original spherical impact is reinterpreted.

Post-build review adds the smooth sliding-friction derivative `mu * normal_load / slip_speed_scale` to near-contact damping/work bounds, including an approaching-foot load estimate. Normal damping alone does not bound this low-speed regularization. Hardware inertia must also fit the authored hull/gear radial extent. New terrain states reject old spherical terminal-impact semantics. Engine kinetic-removal counters describe pre-ejection co-moving/co-rotating material in the body mechanical balance; they do not establish plume/chemical energy or full engine efficiency. Native captures run at a speed preset index (`0` is real time), so a loaded live craft legitimately advances during capture; only recorded branch values are fixed.
